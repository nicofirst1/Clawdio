# Lit review: broadband-noise annoyance in ambient sonification (2026-08-04)

Context: blind round 2 (`research/blind-round2-2026-08-04.md`) found timbre, not density, is the dominant complaint — "white noise and chaos" — despite v2.2's noise-tick drops being a deliberate fix for round 1's "bird call" chirp complaint. This reviews measurable annoyance metrics, calm-tech timbre guidance, and commercial ambient practice, then proposes v2.3 changes.

## 1. Measurable hiss/annoyance metrics

Psychoacoustic annoyance is standardly modeled as a composite of **loudness (N), sharpness (S), roughness (R), and fluctuation strength (FS)**, per Zwicker: `PA = N5[1 + sqrt(w_s^2 + w_FR^2)]`, `w_s = 0.25(S-1.75)log10(N5+10)` for S>1.75, `w_FR = 2.18*N5^0.4*(0.4F + 0.6R)` ([SEA/Internoise 2019 writeup](https://www.sea-acustica.es/INTERNOISE_2019/Fchrs/Proceedings/1503.pdf); [MDPI DNN annoyance paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/9/15/3136)). Full Zwicker loudness/sharpness needs an ISO 532-1 outer/middle-ear filter bank and Bark critical-band integration — heavier than the project wants — but two simplified proxies are directly numpy/scipy-computable from a WAV and target exactly what "hiss" means:

- **Spectral flatness (Wiener entropy)** = geometric mean / arithmetic mean of the power spectrum, computable with `scipy.signal.welch` + two `np.mean` calls (one `np.log` + `np.exp`, one linear). Approaches 1.0 for white noise, 0 for pure tones/harmonic content ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_flatness); [ScienceDirect overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/spectral-flatness)). This is the single most direct proxy for "reads as noise vs. reads as tone" and the cheapest to add — no Bark scale needed.
- **Zwicker sharpness (simplified)**: `S = ∫g(z)N'(z)z dz / ∫N'(z) dz`, a loudness-weighted centroid over Bark rate z, where g(z) is a weighting that is flat (~1) below ~3kHz and rises (~up to 4) above it ([Ansys sharpness doc](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/////////Views/Secured/corp/v242/en/Sound_SAS_UG/Sound/UG_SAS/sharpness_132337.html); [Salford intro](https://acoustics.salford.ac.uk/psychoacoustics/sound-quality-making-products-sound-better/an-introduction-to-sound-quality-testing/sharpness-and-booming/)). Sharpness is explicitly "the perceptual equivalent of spectral centroid," computable the same way but on specific loudness instead of raw magnitude — a coarse but honest proxy is a magnitude-spectrum centroid with a >3kHz weighting boost (`scipy.signal.welch`
  - `np.average` with a `g(z)`-shaped weight vector), avoiding a full Bark transform. Reference unit: 1 acum = narrowband noise at 1kHz/60dB SPL.
- **Roughness/fluctuation strength**: both derive from amplitude-modulation depth of critical-band envelopes (roughness ~15-300Hz mod rate, fluctuation strength <20Hz) via Hilbert-envelope + FFT of the envelope per band ([John D. Cook writeup](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2016/03/30/acoustic-roughness/); [SQAT open-source reference impl, MATLAB](https://github.com/ggrecow/SQAT)). This is computable with `scipy.signal.hilbert` but needs a filterbank (even a coarse 4-8 band one) to be meaningful — more machinery than flatness/sharpness for probably lower payoff here, since the complaint is steady-state hiss, not amplitude-modulated roughness.
- **Tonality / DIN 45681 tone-to-noise ratio**: `TNR` = tone level minus masking-noise level in the surrounding critical band; ECMA-74 calls a tone "prominent" above 8dB TNR (1kHz+, +2.5dB/octave below 1kHz) ([Siemens community explainer](https://community.sw.siemens.com/s/article/tone-to-noise-ratio-and-prominence-ratio); [HEAD acoustics app note](https://cdn.head-acoustics.com/fileadmin/data/global/Application-Notes/SVP/Tonality-Hearing-Model_e.pdf)). This measures the opposite problem (unwanted pure tones) from what's needed here — skip it.
- ISO 12913 mandates loudness (ISO 532-1) and _recommends_ sharpness, roughness, fluctuation strength, tonality "when appropriate" but the standard itself doesn't publish numeric pleasantness target bands for them ([ISO/TS 12913-2](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/449267f1-380f-4de3-a5d4-761a28f7f12b/iso-ts-12913-2-2018); [ISO/TS 12913-3 guide](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/3bc4eb26-3918-4830-af00-e017ed78d6b3/iso-ts-12913-3-2019)) — the project's own `eval/` circumplex scoring is the operative pleasantness signal, not a published numeric sharpness/flatness target from the standard.

**Verdict for `analyze_render.py`**: add **spectral flatness** (direct, cheap, exactly targets "sounds like noise") as a new numeric criterion, and optionally a **simplified sharpness/brightness-above-3kHz weight** as a second. Skip full roughness/fluctuation-strength/TNR — filterbank cost isn't justified when the complaint is steady hiss, not modulation or tonal beating.

## 2. Discrete-event timbre in calm/peripheral audio

Calm technology literature frames the core tension directly: "there is a tension in auditory displays between the sonification being perceptible... and being too intrusive or annoying... the richer the sonification the greater the potential for disturbance" ([Sonification Handbook ch.18, process monitoring](https://sonification.de/handbook/download/TheSonificationHandbook-chapter18.pdf); [sonification.de overview](https://sonification.de/son/)). The "Model of Nature Soundscape for Calm Information Display" paper specifically builds information-bearing ambient audio out of natural, pitched/damped textures (rain, wind, birdsong) rather than synthetic noise bursts, because naturalistic damped-transient timbres are what listeners already tolerate for hours (rain, footsteps, distant traffic) — broadband synthetic noise doesn't have that same cultural/perceptual "free pass" ([IwC 2016 paper](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article-abstract/29/6/813/4084676); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319230768_A_Model_of_Nature_Soundscape_for_Calm_Information_Display)).

Earcon research (the classic auditory-icon/earcon HCI literature) found **structured, musical/tonal earcons outperform unstructured noise bursts** for both learnability and comfort — "earcons were better than unstructured bursts of sound and musical timbres were more effective" ([ACM CHI 2009, earcon vs auditory-icon notifications](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1518701.1518932)) — and separately, guidelines warn against over-intensifying an earcon to the point of annoyance ([BeepBank-500 corpus paper, timbre variables](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17277)). The throughline across both calm-tech and earcon literature: **damped pitched/percussive transients (a struck, resonant, decaying timbre) read as informative-but-tolerable; undamped broadband noise bursts read as alarm/static and fatigue faster**, independent of event rate.

This directly supports the round-2 conclusion: the project's own history (sine chirp → "bird call" complaint; noise tick → "white noise/chaos" complaint) is exactly the earcon-research prediction — pure noise timbres and un-damped narrowband tones are both worse than a **damped, pitched, percussive** timbre (e.g., a short exponentially-decaying resonant filter excited by a noise burst, i.e. a "modal knock" or "woodblock" — which the project already has for `PostToolUseFailure`, just not for the routine rain drops).

## 3. Commercial practice — Endel, Brain.fm, Eno

- **Endel**: sound designer/artist-authored **stems**, not synthesized-noise- only layers — "the app has a huge library of samples and stems, and the algorithm picks the right stems to sequence the audio together," aiming for "less complex tones, melodies, and movement... to ease the burden on our minds" ([Amazon Alexa dev blog, Endel technology writeup](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/blogs/alexa/alexa-skills-kit/2020/06/the-sounds-of-science-how-endels-audio-environments-are-helping-shape-the-future-of-ambient-computing); [Endel technology page](https://endel.io/technology)). No published numeric spectral-centroid target was found (Endel doesn't publish DSP internals) — the qualitative signal is "musical/simple over synthetic/noisy," consistent with the project's own pentatonic-pad choice.
- **Brain.fm**: published white paper + peer-reviewed EEG/fMRI work shows their engine uses **amplitude-modulated musical beds** (not broadband noise) to drive neural phase-locking, and explicitly benchmarks _against_ pink noise as a weaker control condition — "Brain.fm's music... performed better than either silence or pink noise for most people" ([Brain.fm science page](https://www.brain.fm/science); [Brain.fm white paper PDF](https://www.brain.fm/pdfs/white-paper.pdf); [Brain.fm neural phase-locking blog](https://www.brain.fm/blog/how-neural-phase-locking-works)). This is independent supporting evidence that continuous noise beds are _not_ the state of the art for sustained-listening comfort/function even when the goal is background/functional audio, not foreground music.
- **Eno / Bloom**: a generative engine built from a small fixed palette of pitched, decaying piano-like tones triggered sparsely and self-playing when idle — explicitly _not_ a noise-bed architecture; density is capped by the design (few simultaneous voices, long note decays) rather than by a rate limiter on top of a noise generator ([Wikipedia, Bloom software](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom_(software)>); [GenerativeMusic.com, Bloom](https://generativemusic.com/bloom.html); [Tape Op interview](https://tapeop.com/blog/2021/10/30/eno_generative_music_and_bloom)). The project's own melodic "bloom" layer is already explicitly modeled on this — the lesson is to push _more_ of the event vocabulary toward this family and less toward the noise-tick family, not just cap noise-tick rate.

Across all three: none of them, published or reverse-engineered, use a continuous broadband noise layer as a primary texture for hours-long listening. Noise (when present at all — e.g. Brain.fm's pink-noise _control_ condition) is treated as an inferior baseline, not a design target.

## Recommendations for v2.3

1. **Add spectral flatness (Wiener entropy) to `tools/analyze_render.py`** as a new numeric criterion over the steady-state window: `flatness = exp(mean(log(psd))) / mean(psd)` via `scipy.signal.welch`. Target: keep the mixed active-window flatness comfortably below the flatness of the current air-bed/noise-tick material alone (measure the current failing render first to get a concrete baseline number, then set target ~20-30% lower — this project's tools consistently calibrate against a measured positive/negative control rather than a literature-external number, per `tools/complaint_checks.py`'s existing pattern).
2. **Add a simplified sharpness/brightness-above-3kHz ratio** as a second criterion: energy above 3kHz / total energy, weighted per the g(z) pattern. This catches the specific "hiss" register (rain noise-ticks sit 1.8-3.5kHz per the README) independent of overall flatness.
3. **Replace the rain-drop noise-tick timbre with a damped resonant/modal click** (noise burst through a short high-Q bandpass with fast exponential decay, ~5-15ms) rather than a filtered-noise burst with a simple envelope — same "unpitched percussive" read the round-2 notes ask for, but tonally damped instead of broadband, per the earcon-research finding that structured/damped timbres outperform noise bursts. The project already has exactly this timbre family for the failure "knock" (modal ~190Hz) — reuse the same synthesis primitive at a higher, brighter modal frequency (e.g. 1.5-3kHz center) for routine drops instead of building a third timbre family from scratch.
4. **Hard-lowpass or shrink the continuous "air" bed** (per the round-2 decision already made) — corroborated independently by Brain.fm's finding that continuous noise underperforms structured/musical material for sustained listening, and by Eno/Endel's stem-based (not noise-bed) design.
5. Do **not** add roughness/fluctuation-strength/TNR metrics — the complaint is steady-state broadband hiss, which flatness + brightness-ratio already capture; the added filterbank machinery isn't justified yet. Revisit only if a future complaint is specifically about modulation/beating/pulsing rather than "noise."
