## Block C — pacing flip-point probe (same session, 3 rate laws)

All 3 clips render the identical event script (`_pacing_session_events()` in `make_clips.py`): calm reads → busy writes → a hot 12-call Bash burst at 0.35s spacing (activity → ~1.0) → busy edits → Stop. ~34.2 s each. Only the discrete-drop rate-mapping law differs:

| Clip                  | Seed | Rate law                                                                          | Expected behavior during the hot burst                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------- | ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `c1_v2_mapping`       | 301  | v2 original: `rate = 2 + 38·a^1.3` (up to ~40 drops/s)                            | should read as "too fast / losing control" — this is the exact regression the blind listener flagged pre-v2.2; individual drops become uncountable machine-gun noise                                                                   |
| `c2_v22_mapping`      | 302  | v2.2 shipped: compressive log map, capped at 6 drops/s, wash crossfade above ~5/s | should read as "thicker/heavier" during the burst but still controllable/informative — a listener should feel intensity without losing the sense of discrete, countable events at moderate activity and a smooth wash at peak activity |
| `c3_v22_half_density` | 303  | v2.2 map at half density                                                          | should read as calmer/sparser than c2 during the burst, plausibly under-signaling how busy the session actually is — this is the "did we overcorrect" probe                                                                            |

Expected flip-point result: listeners should rank frantic/overwhelming as c1 > c2 > c3, and rank "felt informative about how busy it was" as c2 ≳ c1 > c3 (c1 gives raw information but at the cost of comfort; c3 is comfortable but under-informative; c2 is the intended sweet spot). Use `score.py`'s sign-test helper on the pairwise preference votes (c1 vs c2, c2 vs c3) to check these orderings are statistically supported across listeners, not just a coordinator's assumption.
