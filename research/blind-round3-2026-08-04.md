## Conclusions

1. **Control confirmed worst.** The only discriminating signal — the free comment — singles out S, the current v2.2 noise tick. Round 2's diagnosis ("white noise" complaint is about the noise-tick timbre) reproduces in isolation. This was the primary question and it is answered.
2. **Floor effect — methodology lesson.** All four clips scored 1/1: 25 s of isolated dry taps is unpleasant listening regardless of timbre, so absolute Likert ratings bottomed out and carried no information between P/Q/R. With N=1, absolute ratings are near-useless; only _comparisons_ (forced ranking, pairwise choice) and free comments discriminate. Round 3's rating scales were the wrong instrument; the embedded control + comment saved the round.
3. **No winner among P/Q/R.** Timbre selection must be redone **in context** (candidate timbre embedded in the full ambient mix), not in isolation, and as forced pairwise choice. This folds naturally into round 4: render the same session under v2.3 variants differing only in drop timbre.

## Decision → v2.3

- Drop the noise tick (confirmed twice, sighted + blind-isolated).
- Do NOT pick between marimba/woodblock/plink from this round — carry 2–3 candidates into in-context round 4 as full-mix renders, forced ranking only (no absolute Likert scales for N=1).

## Methodology going forward (in response to "are we being scientific?")

What we do control: single-blind labels, same-session A/B, loudness matching, hidden controls, seeded/reproducible clip generation, results logged per round. What we cannot claim: statistical significance (N=1 listener), absolute scores. Protocol fixes adopted from this round: forced ranking / pairwise choice only; always test in context; always embed a control; free-comment field is mandatory (it has carried the key signal in both rounds).
